    How the rating works

    Each opponent has a rating: the human-like opponents play like Lichess players of that rating, and weakened Stockfish was measured at about 1100 against them. Beating a stronger opponent gains more points than beating a weaker one; losing to a weaker one costs more. Your rating starts at 1500 and moves in bigger steps for the first 10 games.

    A game counts when it is played to the end against one opponent without taking moves back. Hints and comments are fine: they are part of the practice. Games with takebacks are practice games and are not rated. The scale is roughly Lichess’s; FIDE ratings at this level are usually a few hundred points lower.

    A trade is even when both pieces are worth the same, like a bishop for a knight. Giving a bishop or knight for a rook is called winning the exchange: you are 2 points up. The +number beside the captured pieces shows who is ahead. These values are a guide: a well-placed piece can be worth more.
